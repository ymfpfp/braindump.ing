I Biked Japan! I Learned Japanese!

Separated into parts: then, during, and now.

9 Months Before

It is 07/28/2026, a couple days ago (on Sunday) Thomas and I decided that we were going to bike from the tip of the northermost part of Japan, Cape Soya, to the southernmost part of Japan, Cape Sata. This resulted in us excitedly culminating a day-by-day itinerary

For context, Thomas has been to Japan a few times and I have never been. One of my bucket list items is to bike Japan. I'd also like to hang out in Tokyo for a month afterwards.

Something I've been thinking about recently as an experiment is how to learn a language imperatively, i.e. here is a fixed set of steps to take. Given the problem space and possible context beforehand this is not possible, so my next question is if one can come up with some sort of computational way. Each language that we use for communication tends to have the following:

  • Reading

  • Writing

  • Speaking

  • Listening

Reading and writing are notably easier than speaking and listening because they don't have the dimension of time, i.e. you are given as much time as you need. That being said, having voice LLMs means that there are a lot of cool speaking/listening things that you can probably do that you couldn't do before without another human in the picture.

For reading, it is a lot of rote memorization that is made less boring by reading. Geronimo Stilton was the book series in my childhood that made reading fun for me and thus made me into a lifelong reader. I think a good way to do this is have an LLM write a bilingual. Some good transition series for me:

  • Geronimo Stilton

  • Chi's Sweet Home

  • Jules Vernes

  • Harry Potter
